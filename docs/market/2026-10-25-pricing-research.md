# Snapmark: market and pricing research

Date: 2026-10-25. Prices are USD unless stated. Official pricing pages were fetched where possible; where only an aggregator or older source was found, the table says so.

## Summary

1. $1.99 / $4.99 is too low. The cheapest credible Mac screenshot tools sit at $12 (Shottr, pay-what-you-want) to $29.99-$35 (Xnapper, CleanShot X). $19.99 is inside the market; $49.99 is above every one-off comparable.
2. The one-off norm in this category is "pay once, keep the app, get 1 year of updates, renew for about 55-60% of price" (CleanShot X, Xnapper). Buyers already understand it.
3. The AI-feedback niche (Agentation, Vibe Annotations, Vynix, Pinpoint, Drawbridge) is almost all free or open source, and browser/DOM based. I found no commercial native Mac region-capture tool that writes a Markdown session file.
4. Distribution: sell direct through a merchant of record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, 5% + $0.50) rather than the Mac App Store; Setapp (75/25 on single-app one-time sales) is an optional later channel.
5. Recommended: $24.99 one-off with 1 year of updates and a trial, with a Team seat later. Details in section 4.

## 2. The Chrome-extension angle

- **Free is the default.** Vibe Annotations (https://www.vibe-annotations.com/), Agentation (https://www.agentation.com/), Drawbridge, ui-annotate, Browser Annotations and Pinpoint are free or open source. They click an element on a running localhost page and hand the agent the selector, DOM context, often the React component and file:line, via copy-paste or a local MCP server. Source for the grouping: https://github.com/breschio/drawbridge and the other repos in the table.
- **Where money exists, it is per-seat SaaS for teams, not for solo developers.** Vynix Pro from $7/seat/mo (https://www.vynix.in/); Annot $9-$59/mo (https://www.annot.io/blog/best-visual-feedback-tools); Jam.dev Team $14/creator/mo, with MCP also on the free plan (https://jam.dev/pricing); Marker.io $39-$149/mo with an MCP server (https://marker.io/pricing); BugHerd $42-$125/mo, MCP in beta (https://bugherd.com/pricing).
- **Legacy website-feedback tools** (Ruttl, Pastel, Markup.io, Usersnap) are aimed at agencies collecting client comments, priced $18/user to $1,290/yr; per the Annot comparison, Markup.io, Pastel and Ruttl have no MCP. Sources in the table.
- **Agentation** is the most visible: #1 of the day on Product Hunt on 2026-03-27, but free. Source: https://www.producthunt.com/products/agentation
- **Gap.** Every tool above works on a web page in a browser. I found no commercial, native, region-capture-anywhere tool (desktop apps, terminals, simulators) that appends to a session file. The near neighbours are the free GitHub projects Snapback and Show & Tell. That is a differentiator, and also a warning that the core job is cheap to copy.

## 3. Distribution economics

**Mac App Store.** Apple's Small Business Program gives a 15% commission on paid apps while proceeds stay at or under $1M a year; above that the standard rate applies (Apple's page does not state the standard figure; the widely known number is 30%). Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/. Apple is merchant of record. For Snapmark the real costs are not the fee: an Electron app needs a sandboxed MAS build, screen-recording and global-shortcut behaviour must survive the sandbox, and the store has no time-boxed-updates mechanism. The only sources I found on upgrade pricing are from 2013 and say it was unsupported (https://www.engadget.com/2013-08-30-upgrade-pricing-is-the-app-stores-weakness.html); not re-verified for 2026. A "1 year of updates" model does not map onto the store; a flat price or subscription does.

**Setapp.** Two models. Membership: 70% developer / 30% Setapp, revenue shared by usage; of the 30%, 20% goes to referral partners and Setapp keeps 10%. Single-App: Setapp keeps 15% of subscription sales and 25% of one-time sales, and one-time purchases are allowed. Requirements: technical review, Setapp SDK, mature stable software. Source: https://www.setapp.com/developers. Membership pays by usage, so it suits apps opened daily (screenshot tools fit; CleanShot X is on Setapp per https://cleanshot.com/pricing), but a new app with few users earns little. Consumer price is reportedly $14.99/mo for Mac after a June 2026 increase (aggregator: https://pricingsaas.com/companies/setapp; not confirmed on setapp.com, which I could not load).

**Paddle.** 5% + $0.50 per checkout transaction, merchant of record (global tax, fraud, chargebacks, support), no monthly fee. Paddle says sellers with products under $10 should contact them for bespoke pricing. Source: https://www.paddle.com/pricing. At $24.99 the fee is about 7%; at $4.99 it is about 15%.

**Lemon Squeezy.** 5% + $0.50 per transaction, merchant of record (sales tax and VAT included), small extra fees possible outside the US, payouts twice a month. Source: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing. Same economics as Paddle for this use.

**Gumroad.** 10% + $0.50 on direct sales, 30% on sales found through Gumroad Discover; merchant of record since 2025-01-01; no monthly fee. Source: https://gumroad.com/pricing. Cheapest to start, dearest at volume. The $0.50 fixed fee is a heavy tax on a $4.99 product on any of the three.

Net on $24.99 (before payment-method extras): Paddle or Lemon Squeezy about $23.24, Gumroad direct about $21.99, Mac App Store at 15% about $21.24, Setapp one-time at 25% about $18.74.

## 4. Pricing recommendation

**Is $1.99 / $4.99 too low? Yes.**

- The lowest paid comparables are $12 (Shottr, optional) and $29.99-$35 (Xnapper, CleanShot X). Free options exist (Shottr at $0, Zappy, Skitch, Snapback), so the paid floor is already "worth more than free", not "cheaper than a coffee."
- A fixed $0.50 fee takes 25% of $1.99 and 10% of $4.99 on Paddle or Lemon Squeezy; Gumroad takes about 35% and 20%.
- $1.99 signals an unmaintained utility. The buyer is a developer already paying around $20/month for an AI coding tool, and the app saves minutes on every review.
- Support cost per sale is the same at any price.

If the owner meant $19.99 / $49.99: $19.99 is supported; $49.99 is above Xnapper and CleanShot X and is defensible only with a team or Pro feature set. The market supports roughly $19-$35 for a one-off.

### Option A (recommended): $24.99 one-off, 1 year of updates, trial

- Direct sale through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy; license key; the last version works forever; renewal at 60% (Xnapper's structure) or $14.99/yr (just under CleanShot's $19).
- 14-day full trial.
- Reasoning: sits between Shottr ($12) and Xnapper ($29.99), below CleanShot X ($35), inside the "pay once" norm. Fee drag about 7%. The update year funds maintenance without a subscription, which the owner wants to avoid.

### Option B: free tier with a cap, $19.99 Pro unlock

- Free: for example 3 sessions or 25 screenshots a month, Markdown only. Pro one-off $19.99 removes the cap, unlocks PDF/ZIP export and 1 year of updates.
- Reasoning: the AI-feedback competitors are free (Agentation, Vibe Annotations, Vynix free tier), so a cap lowers the barrier, like Shottr or Jam. The cap should bite on sessions, not on the first screenshot. Cost: more free users to support, and the free tier is what Snapback-style clones undercut.

### Option C: $19.99 one-off for individuals, plus a Team seat at about $5-$7 per seat per month

- Team comparables: Xnapper Team $5/device/mo, Vynix Pro $7/seat/mo, CleanShot Pro $10/user/mo. Team would add shared session folders, templates and invoicing.
- Reasoning: build it only once companies ask. The one-off stays as the entry point. Do not launch with it.

### Channel advice

- Launch direct (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy). Skip the Mac App Store for now: sandboxing an Electron screen-capture app, and Option A cannot be expressed there.
- Consider Setapp Single-App later; one-time sales cost 25% there, so it is a reach channel, not the main one. Membership revenue is usage-based and unproven for a new app.
- Position against the free tools on what they cannot do: native region capture anywhere on screen (not only a React dev server), numbered notes plus red/green marks, one Markdown file per session, PDF/ZIP for humans.

## Caveats

- Shottr's $12 figure, Markup Hero, Pastel, Monosnap, Annotely and Setapp consumer prices come from aggregators or app-store listings; the official pages either did not load or did not list them.
- The Mac App Store standard commission (30%) is stated from general knowledge; Apple's small-business page only describes the reduced rate.
- The Mac App Store upgrade-pricing claim is from 2013 articles, not re-checked against current Apple docs.
- Competitor pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool on 2026-10-25. CleanShot's official page showed $35; one aggregator showed $29 (older).
