# Snapmark online: review panel and Chrome extension **What:** the web app's feedback view with a GitHub-style review, and the Chrome extension: side panel, pins on the page, live-page annotation and version compare. **For whom:** product people, designers and engineers reviewing UI changes their coding agents made. They review weekly, sometimes daily. **The job:** see exactly what changed on the running page, talk about it in one thread, and give one verdict (Approve or Request changes) that decides whether a version is accepted. **Source of truth:** `docs/designs/2026-10-27-snapmark-online.md` sections 4.2, 9.1 and 9.5. **Axis the three directions differ on: where the review happens.** - 01 Safe: the web app is home; the extension mirrors it in a side panel (GitHub's model). - 02 Bold: the live page is home; pins and the before/after wipe dominate, the panel is thin. - 03 Contrarian: review is a timeline of versions, not a thread with a button; the verdict is a step at the end of comparing. **Fixed in all three (owner rules):** - The primary button is ALWAYS the last button in its row, at the right. More (…) goes first. Two equal verdicts are no exception. - One filled accent button per region; nothing else uses the accent. - Accept rule: a version is accepted with at least one Approve and no open Request changes. - Annotations sit on the live page, stuck to their element; every version keeps a capture (or a looping recording with filmstrip) so old and new can be compared. **Content (real, from the owner's mock):** workspace "Kestrel Shop", feedback #42 "Total jumps when the coupon applies", status Implemented, versions v0 Original, v1 Mockup · Dana, v2 Implementation · PR #212 · Ben via Claude. 01 Safe

Open live: —

The web app is home: a conventional sidebar, a capture with version tabs, and a chronological thread ending in a pinned review form. Chrome mirrors that panel beside the running page, keeping the same discussion and rightmost commit action. Reviewing and accepting remain separate steps: choose a verdict, submit the review, then accept once there is at least one approval and no open objections. Element-bound diamond markers, local annotation tools, a recording filmstrip and an old/live wipe supply evidence without changing that familiar structure.

02 Bold — The change is the hero

Open live: —

The live element is the place to review: numbered diamond pins lead to a compact thread, while the order summary and its before/after take the largest share of the screen. A four-frame recording makes the jumping total visible; the element-sized wipe brings the old capture onto the running checkout. The web view keeps that same emphasis, with the review form and the separate acceptance step beside the comparison. Quiet system surfaces connect the extension and web app to the Mac app, and every primary action ends its row at the right.

03 Contrarian — Walk the Versions

Open live: —

Review follows a horizontal path from v0 Original through v1 Mockup and v2 Implementation to a final verdict. Adjacent captures take the main stage; comments stay attached to the version they discuss. The extension carries the same path onto the running checkout, with a recording filmstrip for motion, element-attached marks, and a wipe over the saved element. Submitting a review ends the comparison walk; acceptance remains a separate final step once there is at least one approval and no open objection. Calm grey surfaces and familiar Mac controls keep the evidence prominent, with the single blue primary action always last at the right.
