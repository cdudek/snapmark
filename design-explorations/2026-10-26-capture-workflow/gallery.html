01 Safe — Familiar by default

Open live: —

Snapmark follows the macOS Screenshot bar and keeps today’s editor structure: tools across the top, screenshot on the left, and a purpose-built column on the right. Five numbered capture modes sit beside a visible Session / One-Shot choice and session target; conventional Options groups hold recording and timer settings. A large countdown and a compact recording control make timing clear. Session mode gives the general comment and reference notes measured breathing room, a light divider, and an accessible session picker. One-Shot replaces notes with familiar Beautify controls and a clear Copy action that closes the window. Neutral system materials carry the interface, blue identifies app actions, and coral red belongs to annotations.

02 One clear next move

Open live: —

Make the next action impossible to miss. A generous, two-level native capture shelf gives the chosen mode a strong headline and the Capture key a large, isolated button. That hierarchy carries through the oversized countdown, the compact recording control, and the editor’s spacious side column: the work stays large, the destination stays explicit, and the final action owns the blue. Session gives comments and references breathing room; One-Shot gives the same space to a live beautified preview and a decisive Copy action.

03 Destination last

Open live: —

Capture the evidence first; decide its destination at the handoff. The capture bar keeps a compact, editable destination receipt, while the editor moves Session | One-Shot into a fixed delivery dock beside its final action. Notes therefore begin at the top of the column, and changing the destination never interrupts annotation. The capture frame itself carries the countdown and recording controls, so both states stay attached to the area they describe and leave its content visible. One-Shot turns the same column into Beautify before the final Copy closes the editor.
