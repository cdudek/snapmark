# Snapmark: reviewing a recording **The axis:** what the canvas is after a recording. 01 the filmstrip itself is the image you mark; 02 the GIF plays with a scrubber and you mark the moments you stop on; 03 Snapmark shows only the key frames where something changed. Pick one. **What is being designed** (owner, 2026-10-26): Record (⌃⇧5, mode 5) captures an animation, such as a button animation or a loading issue, at 15, 10, 5, 4 or 2 fps, up to 30 s. After Done, the editor opens. A session stores a **filmstrip PNG** (frames in a grid, each labelled with its time) for the AI, because AI models mostly read only a GIF's first frame, plus the **GIF** for people. One-Shot copies or saves the GIF. **The recording in these mockups:** 0:03.2 at 10 fps (32 frames) of the Ledgerly orders page: the table shows a spinner "Loading payments…" for 2.4 s, then the rows appear; the owner's feedback is that the spinner should be a skeleton and the wait is too long. **Fixed:** today's editor (toolbar ⌘1–⌘7, colour swatch, Undo/Redo, side column with General comment, References, Discard ⌘W and the blue "Add to session ⌘↩"); macOS-native, light; app blue #2492FF (filled buttons #0F72DB); annotation marks and numbered diamonds coral red #E5484D. **Two images per direction:** the editor after a recording, with two marks and their notes; and the session window showing that recording's entry next to an ordinary screenshot.
